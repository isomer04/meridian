## B1-1-03 — Allowable age of credit documents

Credit documents must be no more than four months old on the note date. For a tri-merge credit
report this is measured from the date the report was pulled. When a credit report ages beyond
this window a new report is required, and a new report means a new hard inquiry — so a re-pull is
a deliberate act, not an incidental one.

## B2-1.2-01 — Loan-to-value ratios

For a purchase transaction the loan-to-value ratio is calculated using the **lesser of the
property's sales price or its appraised value**. An appraised value above the contract price does
not reduce the LTV; the surplus is not equity and may not be used to support a higher loan
amount. Where the appraised value falls below the contract price, the borrower must either reduce
the loan amount or bring the difference in cash.

## B2-1.2-02 — Combined loan-to-value ratios

The combined loan-to-value ratio includes the subject first lien plus the balance of all
subordinate financing secured by the subject property, whether or not that financing is drawn.
For a HELOC, the full credit line is included unless the line is closed at or before closing.

## B2-1.3-01 — Purchase transactions

A purchase transaction finances the acquisition of the subject property. The sales contract must
be fully executed by all parties. Any sales concession exceeding the applicable limit for the
occupancy and LTV must be deducted from the sales price before the LTV is calculated.

## B2-1.5-02 — Loan eligibility — maximum LTV by occupancy

Maximum loan-to-value ratios for a fixed-rate mortgage on a one-unit property are 97 percent for
a principal residence, 90 percent for a second home, and 85 percent for an investment property.
A loan exceeding the applicable maximum is ineligible for delivery regardless of the borrower's
credit profile or the automated underwriting recommendation.

## B3-3.1-01 — General income information

Income used to qualify must be stable, predictable and reasonably expected to continue for at
least three years. The lender must document both the amount and the likelihood of continuance.
Income that is declining requires additional analysis, and a declining trend may not be averaged
in a way that overstates the borrower's capacity.

## B3-3.1-02 — Standards for employment documentation

For a salaried borrower, obtain a paystub dated within 30 days of the application date showing
year-to-date earnings, plus W-2 forms for the most recent two years. A verbal verification of
employment must be obtained within ten business days prior to the note date.

## B3-3.1-03 — Base pay, bonus and overtime income

Base pay is used at the borrower's current annualized rate; it is not averaged. Bonus and
overtime are variable income and must be averaged over the period they have been received, with a
two-year history preferred. Where variable income is declining year over year, the more recent
and lower figure must be used rather than the average.

## B3-3.1-09 — Other sources of income — rental income

Rental income from a property not owned by the borrower for a full tax year is calculated using
75 percent of the gross monthly rent shown on the lease, the remaining 25 percent accounting for
vacancy and maintenance.

## B3-3.2-01 — Underwriting factors for self-employment income

A borrower with an ownership interest of 25 percent or more is self-employed. Qualifying income
is determined through a **cash-flow analysis** of the business returns, not by reading a single
line from a tax return. A two-year history of self-employment is normally required; a shorter
history may be considered only where the borrower has prior comparable employment in the same
field and the business shows sufficient stability.

## B3-3.2-02 — Business structures and required documentation

Obtain two years of signed individual federal returns with all schedules, and business returns
where the business files separately. For a sole proprietorship the analysis begins with Schedule
C net profit. For a partnership or S corporation, begin with the K-1 ordinary business income
attributable to the borrower.

## B3-3.4-01 — Analyzing partnership and S corporation returns

Add back non-cash expenses that reduce net profit without reducing cash: depreciation,
depletion, amortization, casualty loss, business use of the home, and the non-deductible portion
of meals and entertainment. Subtract items that consume cash but do not appear as an expense,
including non-recurring income and obligations coming due within twelve months.

## B3-5.1-01 — General requirements for credit reports

A tri-merge report from all three national repositories is required. The **representative score**
is the middle of the three scores returned for a borrower. Where there are multiple borrowers,
the representative score for the loan is the **lowest** of the borrowers' representative scores.

## B3-5.1-02 — Credit inquiries

All inquiries within the 120 days preceding the credit report date must be addressed. Multiple
mortgage inquiries within a rate-shopping window are treated as a single inquiry for scoring
purposes, but **each inquiry remains recorded on the consumer's credit report**.

## B3-6-02 — Debt-to-income ratios

The debt-to-income ratio is the borrower's total monthly qualifying obligations divided by total
monthly qualifying income. The maximum allowable ratio for a loan underwritten through Desktop
Underwriter is 50 percent; a manually underwritten loan is limited to 36 percent, or 45 percent
with the required credit score and reserves.

## B3-6-03 — Monthly housing expense for the subject property

The monthly housing expense includes principal and interest, property taxes, hazard insurance,
mortgage insurance, and any homeowners' association or co-op assessment. A leasehold or ground
rent payment is included where applicable.

## B3-6-05 — Monthly debt obligations

Include the monthly payment for all installment debt, the greater of the stated minimum payment
or five percent of the outstanding balance for a revolving account with no reported minimum, and
any court-ordered obligation. An installment debt with fewer than ten monthly payments remaining
may be excluded. A debt for which the borrower is an authorized user only may be excluded on
evidence that another party has made the payments.

## B3-6-07 — Debts paid off or closed at or before closing

A debt satisfied at or before closing may be excluded from the ratio provided the source of funds
is documented and the payoff is reflected on the closing disclosure. For a deferred student loan
with no payment reported, use one half of one percent of the outstanding balance as the monthly
payment.

## B3-4.1-01 — Minimum reserve requirements

Reserves are measured in months of the qualifying monthly housing expense. A second home
requires a minimum of two months of reserves. Reserves may not include the proceeds of the
subject transaction or gift funds not yet received.

## B4-1.2-01 — Appraisal report forms

A full interior and exterior appraisal on the applicable report form is required unless the loan
casefile has received an offer of value acceptance through Desktop Underwriter. The appraiser
must report at least three closed comparable sales.

## B4-1.4-10 — Value acceptance (formerly appraisal waiver)

**Value acceptance** replaces the term "appraisal waiver," which has been eliminated from this
Guide. Value acceptance is an **offer issued by Desktop Underwriter** on eligible loan casefiles
based on Fannie Mae's property and market data. A lender may not create a value acceptance from
its own loan-to-value analysis; the offer must be present in the DU findings.

Exercising an offer is at the lender's option and the lender remains responsible for the value.
An offer **must not be exercised** where the lender has reason to believe the estimated value is
unsupported, where the property has been materially affected by a disaster, or where the
property's condition or uniqueness makes the data-driven estimate unreliable. Where an offer is
declined the lender obtains a full appraisal and the loan is delivered on the appraised value.

## B4-1.3-06 — Property condition and quality ratings

A property rated C5 or C6 for condition, or Q6 for quality, is not eligible until the deficiency
is cured and the appraiser reports the completed condition. Ratings of C1 through C4 are
generally acceptable.

## B5-1-01 — High-balance mortgage loan eligibility

A high-balance loan exceeds the general conforming loan limit for the number of units in the
county where the property is located. High-balance loans carry reduced maximum LTV ratios and are
not eligible for certain product features.

## B7-1-01 — Mortgage insurance coverage requirements

Mortgage insurance is required on any loan with an LTV above 80 percent. Required coverage varies
with the LTV band and the loan term. Coverage must be in force at delivery, and evidence of the
commitment must be in the file prior to closing.

## E-3-02 — Acronyms and glossary of defined terms

**AUS** — automated underwriting system. **DU** — Desktop Underwriter. **LPA** — Loan Product
Advisor. **PTD/PTF/PTC** — conditions to be satisfied prior to docs, prior to funding, and prior
to closing respectively. **Value acceptance** — the offer described in B4-1.4-10, replacing the
retired term "appraisal waiver."
